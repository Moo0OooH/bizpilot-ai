# V3 Product Truth and Positioning

Date: 2026-07-13

Status: canonical strategy input for V3 copy and implementation

## The user problem

Service-business owners, sales managers, and support staff receive customer questions through websites, social profiles, Google Business, email, text, phone follow-up, and direct messages. The problem is not only where a message appears. The message itself is usually incomplete:

- "How much?"
- "Are you available Friday?"
- "Do you cover my area?"
- "Can you quote this?"

At higher volume, the team repeatedly asks for scope, location, timing, access, photos, property details, and other service-specific context. The delay comes from turning a vague question into a request that can be answered responsibly.

## What BizPilot does now

1. The business creates one Smart Intake Link.
2. It shares that link anywhere customers already reach the business: website, social bio, saved reply, Google Business Profile, email signature, QR code, or a direct message.
3. The link asks a short set of service-specific questions.
4. BizPilot organizes the answers into a clear request.
5. It flags what is still missing.
6. AI assists with a summary and reply draft inside approved business context.
7. A human reviews, edits, copies, and sends the response through the real customer channel.

## What BizPilot does not do now

- It is not a direct Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, email, SMS, or Google Business inbox integration.
- It does not ingest scattered conversations automatically.
- It does not send messages automatically.
- It does not invent a price or issue an automatic quote.
- It does not book appointments.
- It does not invoice or process payments.
- It is not a full CRM.
- It does not claim validated production readiness for service verticals beyond the approved pilot evidence.

## Primary audience

### Decision maker

An owner or operating manager at a local service business who is still close to customer requests and feels the cost of repeated follow-up.

### Daily user

The owner, sales lead, office coordinator, dispatcher, or support teammate who must determine what a customer wants and prepare the next responsible answer.

### Current validated entry point

Cleaning services are the first end-to-end pilot example. The product foundation may later support additional service categories, but the site must label them as future templates until validated.

## Positioning statement

For busy service teams that receive vague, inconsistent customer questions, BizPilot provides one shareable Smart Intake Link that asks the right questions, organizes each request, shows what is missing, and prepares a reply for human review. Unlike an omnichannel inbox or autonomous agent, BizPilot works alongside the channels the business already uses and keeps the final response under human control.

## Short positioning

> One link across every customer touchpoint. Complete requests. Faster human-approved replies.

## Message hierarchy

1. Pain: customer questions are scattered and incomplete.
2. Mechanism: share one Smart Intake Link wherever customers contact you.
3. Product action: ask the right service questions and organize the answers.
4. Outcome: complete requests, visible gaps, and replies ready to review.
5. Trust: nothing sends without a person.

## Hero visual truth contract

### Stage 1 — scattered questions

Show short bubbles labelled by where customers contact the business. A nearby plain-language label must make clear that these are placement/contact examples, not live inbox integrations.

### Stage 2 — one intake link

Show the saved reply or link card and two or three large service questions. The form is the bridge between a vague message and a usable request.

### Stage 3 — ready for the team

Show the organized request, a visible missing/completed state, a short reply draft, and Review/Edit/Copy. Do not show an active automatic Send action.

## Claim review checklist

Before any public copy ships, answer yes to all:

- Does the copy describe link placement rather than direct channel ingestion?
- Does the copy keep human review and manual sending explicit?
- Does it avoid automatic price, booking, payment, and CRM implications?
- Is cleaning the only vertical described as pilot-ready?
- Are all metrics, testimonials, logos, and customer outcomes omitted unless verified?
- Can a visitor understand the pain, mechanism, and outcome in five seconds?
- Does the French copy read naturally in Canadian/Quebec French rather than mirror English word-for-word?

## Conversion model

- Primary educational action: see how the workflow works.
- Primary commercial action: apply for the founder pilot.
- No self-serve checkout claim.
- No production customer-data submission in public demos.
- Pilot contact must use the safe mechanism already supported by the product; no empty-recipient placeholder link.

## Strategic boundary

The site can present BizPilot as a focused foundation that may expand across service businesses. It cannot market future functionality as current. Ambition belongs in a concise roadmap/FAQ context; the homepage should sell the current pain-to-solution path.
